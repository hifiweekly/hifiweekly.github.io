GLM-4.6V · glm46v.ptx · one warp per head, one butterfly shuffle

Inside bias_rope_kv

GLM-4's q, k and v projections have biases, and its rotary embedding turns only the first 64 of each head's 128 dims, in NeoX pairs: dim i with dim i + 32. This kernel adds the bias to every head of a token's qkv row, rotates the query and key heads, writes q back in place and k, v to the f16 KV cache. It replaces Llama 3.1's rope_kv, which rotates adjacent pairs over the whole head: here a pair's two values sit 32 floats apart, in the registers of lanes l and l ^ 8, so the warp swaps them with one shfl.bfly, and half of the warp only adds the bias.

x ← x + b;  (x'i, x'i+32) = (xi cos θi − xi+32 sin θi,  xi sin θi + xi+32 cos θi), i < 32   // θi = pos · 500000−i/32; dims 64–127 keep x + b

36 warps per token: bias in every lane, rotation in half of them

The grid is (32 + 2 + 2, tokens) blocks of one warp each. Block h reads head h of the token's qkv row (every head is 128 floats at 128·h) and the same 128 floats of the bias vector, and lane l takes dims 4l to 4l + 3. For a query or key head, lanes 0–7 hold the first halves xi of the 32 rotary pairs, lanes 8–15 the second halves xi+32, and lanes 16–31 the 64 dims that carry no position. Why it is built this way

    

      

        
NeoX pairs: one shuffle per value

        
With 4 consecutive dims per lane (one 16-byte ld.v4.f32; the warp's 32 loads cover the 512-byte head in one pass), dim i of a pair is in lane i / 4 and dim i + 32 in lane i / 4 + 8: the two lanes differ in bit 3 of the lane index. shfl.sync.bfly.b32 … 8 hands every lane the value of lane l ^ 8, four shuffles for the four registers, and lanes 0–7 then compute xi cos − xi+32 sin while lanes 8–15 compute xi+32 cos + xi sin. The sign is the only difference, applied by four predicated negs of the sines, so both halves run the same multiply and FMA.

        
The shuffles come before the branch that sends lanes 16–31 away: a shfl.sync with the full mask must be executed by all 32 lanes. Lanes 16–31 shuffle among themselves (16 ↔ 24, …) and throw the result away. Qwen 3.5's qk_norm_rope rotates the same 64 dims with 8 dims per lane and a l ^ 4 partner; Llama 3.1's rope_kv needs no shuffle because its pairs are adjacent.

        
The bias, fused

        
The matrix kernels can add a vector, but it is the residual input resid[t][r], one per token; a bias is the same for every token. Rather than a launch that reads and writes the 4608-float row once more, the kernel that already holds the row adds it: one more ld.global.nc.v4.f32 per lane, 512 bytes per head, read from L2 by every token. The bias goes on all 128 dims of all 36 heads, v included, before the rotation, as llama.cpp's build_qkv adds it to the projection output.

      

      

        
Half of the head rotates: M-RoPE

        
GLM-4.6V is a vision-language model. Its RoPE is multimodal: rope.dimension_sections = [8, 12, 12, 0] gives pairs 0–7 the token's temporal position, pairs 8–19 its row in an image and pairs 20–31 its column. For a text token all three are its position in the sequence, so the angles are those of ordinary RoPE, θi = pos · 500000−2i/64, with the 64 rotated dims in NeoX order (llama.cpp runs M-RoPE in its NeoX layout). rope.dimension_count = 64: dims 64–127 get no rotation and carry content only. The host's rope_table() builds cos/sin for 32 pairs per position (256 bytes); an image would need a table per section, which this text-only engine does not have.

        
Two key/value heads

        
Of the 36 blocks, 32 rotate a query head and store it back, 2 rotate a key head into the K cache, and 2 copy a value head into the V cache. With only 2 kv heads a position takes 2 × 128 × 2 bytes of K and as much of V per layer: 1 KiB, 40 KiB over 40 layers, a third of Llama 3.1's 128 KiB, so 8,192 positions fit in 320 MiB. attention then lets 16 query heads share each cached head.
