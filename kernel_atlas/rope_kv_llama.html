Inside rope_kv (Llama)

Right after the stacked q, k, v projection, Llama 3.1 rotates every query and key head by angles that depend on the token's position (rotary position embedding, RoPE), and the token's keys and values go into the f16 KV cache. This kernel does both. Unlike gpt-oss's rope_kv, it rotates adjacent dimensions (2i with 2i + 1), and the angles carry Llama 3's long-context scaling: the slowest pairs turn up to 8 times slower. The scaling lives in the host's cos/sin table, so the kernel is four multiplies and two stores per pair.

(x'2i, x'2i+1) = (x2i cos θi − x2i+1 sin θi,  x2i sin θi + x2i+1 cos θi)   // θi = pos · 500000−i/64 / fi, fi from rope_freqs.weight

48 warps per token: rotate, rotate and cache, copy

The grid is (32 + 8 + 8, tokens) blocks of one warp each. Block h reads head h of the token's qkv row (every head, q, k or v, is 128 floats at 128·h), and lane l takes dims 4l to 4l + 3: rotary pairs 2l and 2l + 1, both in its own registers. Query heads are rotated in place; key heads are rotated and written to the K cache; value heads are only converted to f16 for the V cache. Click a head, then a lane.

Why it is built this way

    

      

        
Adjacent pairs: no shuffles

        
ggml's "normal" RoPE rotates dim 2i with dim 2i + 1. A lane that loads 4 consecutive floats (one ld.v4.f32, 16 bytes; the warp's 32 loads cover the 512-byte head in one pass) therefore holds both partners of two pairs, and needs nothing from any other lane. Compare the kernels that rotate NeoX pairs (i, i + d/2): Gemma 3's qk_norm_rope and Qwen's version exchange halves with shfl.sync, and gpt-oss's rope_kv gives each lane one pair and loads it with two strided accesses.

        
Hugging Face's Llama code rotates NeoX-style. The GGUF converter permutes the rows of attn_q and attn_k so that the projection's output dims come out interleaved; adjacent-pair RoPE on the permuted output equals NeoX RoPE on the original, and q·k is unchanged because q and k are permuted the same way.

      

      

        
The factors are in the table

        
Llama 3.1 was trained at 8,192 tokens. A pair whose wavelength 2π / ωi is under 2,048 tokens (pairs 0–28) keeps its frequency; one whose wavelength exceeds 8,192 (pairs 35–63) is slowed 8×, so positions up to 131,072 stay within the angles seen in training; pairs 29–34 blend the two by how many wavelengths fit in 8,192. the GGUF converter computes the 64 factors from the model's rope_scaling settings and stores them as rope_freqs.weight, and rope_table() in llama31.c divides each angle by it, building cos/sin for every position up to the context length (512 bytes per position).

        
The kernel then reads one v4.f32 per lane, {cos, sin} of both its pairs, and does no transcendental math. There is no magnitude scale (unlike gpt-oss's YaRN factor 1.347): the rotation keeps every vector's length.
