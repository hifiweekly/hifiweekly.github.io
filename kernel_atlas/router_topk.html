Inside router_topk

gpt-oss sends each token through 4 of its 32 experts. router_topk takes the 32 router logits from mv_f32, picks the 4 largest and turns them into mixing weights with a softmax over just those 4. With 32 experts and 32 lanes, each lane holds one expert's logit, and the whole selection happens in registers: four rounds of a warp-wide argmax, each removing its winner.

ids = top-4 of logit[0…31]    wj = exp(logitids[j] − m) / Σk<4 exp(logitids[k] − m)   // m = the largest; ties go to the smaller expert

Four rounds of argmax, then a softmax

Each round, every lane offers its (logit, expert) pair to a five-step butterfly that keeps the larger logit, or the smaller expert on a tie. After the round all lanes agree on the winner; lane j stores the j-th pick and the winning lane replaces its logit with −∞ so it cannot win again.

Why it is built this way

    

      

        
Selection instead of sorting

        
Only 4 of 32 values are needed, so a full sort would be wasted work. Four argmax rounds cost 4 × 5 shuffle pairs (value and index), all in registers, and the picks land in lanes 0–3 already in descending order. That order matters downstream: moe_combine and the routing log see slot 0 as the strongest expert.

        
The tie rule (equal logits → smaller expert number) makes the choice deterministic regardless of shuffle order, the same rule argmax uses for the vocabulary.

      

      

        
Softmax over the chosen four only

        
gpt-oss normalizes after selecting, so the four weights always sum to 1. The maximum is lane 0's pick (the first round's winner), fetched with one shfl.sync.idx. Lanes 4–31 still hold −∞ as their "pick", so exp gives exactly 0 and the 32-lane butterfly sum counts only the four real terms: no special cases.

        
exp is ex2.approx of (logit − max) · log₂e; the division is IEEE div.rn.
