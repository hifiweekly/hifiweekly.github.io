SAM 3 · sam3.ptx · one thread per (token, head, pair), table-driven 2-D rotation

Inside rope_vit

Between the ViT's qkv product and its attention, this kernel does three jobs for every token. It splits the f32 qkv row into per-head q, k and v. It rotates q and k with a 2-D axial rotary embedding: the first 16 pairs of each 64-dim head turn by the token's column, the last 16 by its row, so the attention scores depend on the horizontal and vertical offsets between two patches. And it writes them as f16 rows in the layout flash_attn reads, window by window: in the 28 windowed blocks each 24 × 24 window becomes a sequence of 576 tokens of its own, in the 4 global blocks the 5184 tokens form one sequence.

(q′2j, q′2j+1) = (q2j cos θj − q2j+1 sin θj,  q2j sin θj + q2j+1 cos θj),  θj = (j < 16 ? x : y) · s · 10000−(j mod 16)/16   // s = 1 in a window, 24/72 globally; k alike, v copied

2.65 million threads: every token, head and pair

The ViT works on the 72 × 72 grid of 14-pixel patches. A thread takes one rotary pair (2 of the 64 dims) of one head of one token. It reads 2 floats each of q, k and v from the token's qkv row (3072 floats), looks up the pair's cos and sin for the token's position, and writes 3 × 2 halves. Why it is built this way

    

      

        
Two axes in one head

        
Ordinary RoPE turns pair j of a head by pos · ωj, so q·k depends on the difference of two positions in a sequence. An image has two coordinates. SAM 3's ViT (from Meta's Perception Encoder, after rope-vit) gives the first half of each head's pairs the column and the second half the row, with the same 16 frequencies 10000−i/16 for both. The score of two patches then depends on their horizontal and vertical offsets separately. The pairs are adjacent dims (2j, 2j + 1), the "interleaved" layout of torch.view_as_complex, so a thread needs no shuffle. The 2 floats it reads are next to each other.

        
The global blocks extrapolate by scaling

        
The RoPE was trained on a 24 × 24 grid (336-pixel images); at 1008 pixels there are 72 positions per axis. In a windowed block that is no problem: a window is 24 × 24, and positions run 0 … 23 inside it. The 4 global blocks see the whole 72 × 72 grid, and their positions are multiplied by 24/72 (use_interp_rope), so they stay in the 0 … 24 range the frequencies were trained on, at a third of a step per patch. The host builds both cos/sin tables once (rope_table(): 576 and 5184 positions × 32 pairs), in f32 exactly as the PyTorch code computes its freqs_cis, and the kernel takes the one for the block.

      

      

        
The window partition happens here

        
A windowed block attends within each 24 × 24 window. PyTorch reshapes the [72][72][1024] activations into 9 windows of [24][24] before attention and back after. Here the reshape costs nothing extra: the thread computes the token's window b = (y / 24) · 3 + x / 24 and position l = (y % 24) · 24 + x % 24, and writes q, k, v to row l of sequence b · 16 + h. flash_attn then sees 144 independent sequences of 576 tokens, and its out_token undoes the partition when it stores the output. A global block writes 16 sequences of 5184.

        
Memory bound by design

        
The kernel reads the 64 MB f32 qkv buffer and writes 32 MB of f16 heads: about 0.2 ms per block, near the GPU's 504 GB/s, for 6.5 ms per image (4.5% of the GPU time). Fusing it into the qkv GEMM's epilogue would save that traffic, but the epilogue writes C row by row in tiles, not in the per-window, per-head layout attention needs.
