Inside mv_q5_k

The DeltaNet output projection (ssm_out, 4096 × 4096 in each of the 24 DeltaNet layers) is stored in Q5_K, one of ggml's "k-quants". Where Q4_1 gives every 32 values an f16 scale and offset, Q5_K groups 256 values into a super-block with one f16 scale d and one f16 dmin, and gives each of its 8 sub-blocks a 6-bit scale and a 6-bit min, packed into 12 bytes. Weights are 5 bits: a nibble plus one bit from a shared 32-byte array. That is 5.5 bits per weight with finer scales than Q5_0. This kernel unpacks it without a single branch.

weight = d·scs·q − dmin·ms  ⇒  block term = (d·scs)·dₓ · Σ q·x − (dmin·ms) · dₓΣx   // q in 0…31; sc, m in 0…63; s = sub-block 0…7

One lane, one sub-block of 32

Lane b takes sub-block b of the row, so its position in the super-block is s = b mod 8: eight lanes share each super-block. Pick the sub-block and step through the 176-byte layout, the scale bits, the 5-bit values and the dot product.

Lanes over the sub-blocks of a row

      
A row of 4096 values is 16 super-blocks, 128 sub-blocks. Lane l takes sub-blocks l, l + 32, l + 64, l + 96: four rounds, and in every round the warp covers 4 whole super-blocks, lanes 8k … 8k + 7 sharing one. Click a cell to inspect it above.

    

    

      
      

      

    

  


  

    
Why it is built this way

    

      

        
The blocks stay whole, the lanes share them

        
176 bytes is a multiple of 16, so unlike Q4_1 and Q8_0 the super-blocks are uploaded as stored and every load is an aligned v4. Each lane loads the 16-byte header, the 32 bytes of qh and the 32 bytes of qs its sub-block uses: 80 bytes for 22 bytes of its own data. The 8 lanes of a super-block read the same header and qh, and pairs read the same qs bytes (even s takes low nibbles, odd s high), so the repeats are served by L1. From DRAM each byte comes once: the kernel runs at about 445 GB/s.

      

      

        
No branches on s

        
The 6-bit scale and min have two encodings (s < 4: the low 6 bits of bytes s and s + 4; s ≥ 4: a nibble of byte s + 4 plus the top 2 bits of bytes s − 4 and s). The kernel computes both with bfe (bit-field extract, with the bit position in a register) and picks one with selp: no divergence, since lanes of different s run side by side. The fifth bits are bit s of each qh byte: one shift by s and a mask of 0x01010101 extracts four at once.

        
The min enters as −dmin·m times dₓΣx, the same one-FMA trick as the offset in mv_q4_1.
