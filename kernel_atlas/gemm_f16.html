Inside gemm_f16

Every matrix product of SAM 3 runs in this one kernel: the ViT's qkv, projection and MLP products, the text encoder's, the fusion encoder's and decoder's, every convolution of the neck and the pixel decoder (as an im2col matrix), and the final product of 200 mask embeddings with 82,944 pixel embeddings. It computes C = A · BT with A the f16 activations [M][K] and B the weights [N][K] exactly as a PyTorch Linear stores them, on the tensor cores: mma.sync.m16n8k16 takes a 16 × 16 f16 tile of A and a 16 × 8 tile of B and adds their product into a 16 × 8 tile of f32 accumulators. Around it are the three things that keep the tensor cores fed: a 3-stage cp.async ring that copies the next k-tiles while this one is computed, a swizzled shared-memory layout that ldmatrix reads without bank conflicts, and an epilogue that applies the bias and the next operation (GELU, ReLU, a residual add) as it stores.

C[m][n] = mode( Σk A[m][k] · B[n][k] + bias[n] )   // f16 × f16 → f32; mode: f32 · f16 · GELU · ReLU · += C · ReLU f32

A 128 × 128 tile per block, 64 × 32 per warp, 16 × 8 per mma

The grid has one block per 128 × 128 tile of C. Its 8 warps split the tile 2 × 4: warp w owns rows 64·(w & 1) … +63 and columns 32·(w >> 1) … +31, which is 4 × 4 mma tiles of 16 × 8, 64 f32 accumulators per thread. The k dimension goes by in k-tiles of 32 (two mma steps of 16). Pick a product, then click a warp's tile, or a lane in the fragment view.

Why it is built this way

    

      

        
f16 in, f32 out

        
The GGUF holds f32 weights; the engine converts them to f16 once at load (f32_to_f16) and halves both the memory (1.6 GB on the GPU) and the bytes every product reads. The inputs of every product are f16 as well: LayerNorm, GELU, ReLU and the attention kernel write f16 copies directly. f16 keeps 11 significant bits; the reference run with --ranges found the largest value entering any product to be 214, far below f16's 65,504, so nothing overflows. The accumulators are f32, and so are the residual streams that mode 4 adds into: the error of one product stays at f16 rounding of its inputs, about 5 · 10−4 relative.

        
The ring of three stages

        
cp.async.cg.shared.global copies 16 bytes from global to shared memory without passing through registers, and returns immediately; cp.async.commit_group closes a group of them and cp.async.wait_group n waits until at most n groups are still in flight. The prologue issues k-tiles 0 and 1. In each iteration a thread waits until only one group is pending (k-tile kt has landed), the block synchronizes, it issues k-tile kt + 2 into the stage that k-tile kt − 1 used, and computes k-tile kt. Two copies are always in flight under one tile of mma work. The barrier at the top of the iteration also guarantees that no warp still reads the stage being refilled. Groups are committed even when there is nothing left to load, so the counting stays the same at the end.

        
Every product of the model in one kernel

        
The epilogue mode covers all the uses: mode 0 for q, k, v (f32, for rope_vit and split_heads), 1 for f16 inputs of the next product, 2 for fc1 + GELU, 3 for the ReLU feed-forwards, 4 for every output projection that adds into the residual stream, 5 for the box head's f32 ReLU. Convolutions become products: the patch embedding and the 3 × 3 layers through im2col (im2col_patch, im2col3), the 1 × 1 layers directly, the 2 × 2 stride-2 deconvolutions as a product to 4 · out columns followed by depth2space. The requirements are N % 128 = 0 and K % 32 = 0, which every SAM 3 matrix meets once the 588-wide patch rows are padded to 608.

      

      

        
The swizzle

        
A shared-memory row of the stage is 32 halves, 64 bytes, four 16-byte chunks. ldmatrix reads an 8 × 8 matrix as 8 rows of 16 bytes, one address per lane of a group of 8 lanes. Stored plainly, rows r and r + 2 are 128 bytes apart and fall in the same 4 banks: the 8 rows of one phase would hit 2 bank groups, a 4-way conflict. Storing chunk c of row r at chunk c ^ ((r >> 1) & 3) spreads the 8 rows over the 8 bank groups of a 128-byte line (the figure above). The loader writes with the same XOR, so the data is where the reads expect it. Within any 16-row group the swizzle depends only on the lane, so each thread computes it once for all its fragments.

        
ldmatrix and the fragments

        
ldmatrix.x4 loads four 8 × 8 matrices at once and hands every lane two halves of each, laid out exactly as mma wants its operands: for A, lanes 0–15 give the rows of k 0–7 and lanes 16–31 the rows of k 8–15, which makes registers a0 … a3 (rows g and g + 8, k 2t … 2t + 1 and + 8). B is stored [n][k], the "col" layout of mma.row.col, so the same non-transposed ldmatrix gives b0, b1 for two n-tiles. Per k16 step a warp issues 4 loads for A, 2 for B and 16 mma; the A fragments are reused across 4 n-tiles and the B fragments across 4 m-tiles.

        
What it reaches

        
On the RTX 4070 SUPER the ViT's products reach about 50 TFLOPS (4.6 TFLOP of products per image in under 0.1 s, nsys). gemm_f16 is 67% of the engine's GPU time; flash_attn is most of the rest. It has no split-k and computes whole 128-row tiles even for the decoder's 201 rows and the text encoder's 32 (rows past M are computed and dropped), which costs little next to the image products.
