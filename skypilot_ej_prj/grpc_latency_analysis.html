Which launch phases gRPC could shorten #

Short answer: mostly phases D, E and F. For a 6-node sky jobs launch on kind, those phases spend up to about 8.4 s on head commands that SKYPILOT_ENABLE_GRPC=1 would replace, out of a 35–41 s launch. A realistic gain is about 5–7 s, but that is an estimate: no sky jobs launch latency has been measured with gRPC on.

Sources:

  • Timings: the ej-prj-i2-5 wait trace, bench_results/20261102-wait-trace/full/analysis.txt (11 traced launches, commit 1ffd36f8f). The phase letters are the ones in launch_latency_consolidated_design.md.
  • gRPC paths: the is_grpc_enabled_with_flag checks in the code on ej-prj-i2-6.
  • Earlier measurement: launch_latency_analysis_4_nodes.md §6.

Phases gRPC would shorten #

Without gRPC, each head command is a kubectl exec plus a Python start, about 1.6 s. Times are the average per launch across the traced launches. A command can span two phases, so it can appear in more than one row.

Phase Head commands gRPC would replace Time spent
D: provisioning → submitted set_autostop 1.58 s, _add_job 1.32 s ~2.9 s
E: job runs queue job (_exec_code_on_head) 1.59 s, is_definitely_autostopping 1.19 s, the rest of _add_job 0.26 s ~3.0 s
F: job ended → RUNNING status and times check (_job_status_and_times_on_head) 1.62 s, is_definitely_autostopping 0.81 s ~2.4 s

Phases gRPC wouldn't help #

  • A, B and C: the job's cluster doesn't exist yet. The run_on_head call in B is the scheduler starting locally (_consolidated_launch), not a call to the cluster.
  • G: already about 0.1 s.
  • H: its one regular head command, the log directory lookup in sync_down_logs (1.55 s in the trace), was removed by change 7 on ej-prj-i2-6. What remains is the wait for saved logs and the log copy, which still uses kubectl exec.
  • The rest of D: pod start, Ray and runtime setup don't go through these calls. They make up most of D's roughly 15 s.

Why the real gain will be under 8.4 s #

  • Opening the connection has a cost. In the earlier test, job submission took 1.4 s on the first launch and 0.4–0.5 s on the next two. That analysis attributed the difference to opening the skylet tunnel, without confirming it. Every managed job launches a new cluster, so some launches may pay this.
  • E may not shrink by the full 3 s. I didn't check whether the 1.19 s is_definitely_autostopping call blocks the job or runs alongside it. Only the 1.59 s queue step clearly delays the job's start.
  • gRPC calls aren't free. Status and times stay two separate, cheap calls in F.
  • The trace is of ej-prj-i2-5, not ej-prj-i2-6. Changes 6 and 7 are described as affecting phase H, so D, E and F should be about the same, but this wasn't traced on ej-prj-i2-6.

What has been measured #

Only plain sky launch, on an older branch with 4 nodes (launch_latency_analysis_4_nodes.md §6, 3 runs):

Metric Baseline gRPC Δ
Job submission (Backend.execute) 3.68 s 0.48 s −3.2 s
"Cluster launched" → client returns 11.7 s 8.2 s −3.5 s

No fallback to SSH was seen in those runs. The 4 launches with gRPC on in launch_latency_design_after_job2.md §8.2 checked recorded job durations, not latency.

Related notes #

  • launch_latency_design_after_job2.md §9 item 5 undercounts gRPC. It lists gRPC only against the head commands around submission. The trace shows it would also cover the is_definitely_autostopping calls and F's status check. Item 4 there, skipping is_definitely_autostopping after the controller's own launch, overlaps with this, so the two gains shouldn't be added.
  • Before making gRPC the default: find out why SKYPILOT_ENABLE_GRPC is off by default, and test clusters whose skylet tunnel can't be reached, to check the fallback to SSH (from launch_latency_analysis_4_nodes.md §6).
